# Portfolio analytics

TradePilot computes portfolio figures deterministically from Trading 212 data, so the model does not have to do arithmetic. All of these tools are read-only, except that `manage_history_export` can request a report and `manage_trade_journal` and `manage_watchlist` write to TradePilot's own store.

## The holdings map

The dashboard shows the holdings as a table or as a map, and remembers the choice per viewer where the host allows local storage.

- **The table** keeps Trading 212's order until you sort it, from the **Sort** menu or by clicking a column header: value or profit or loss largest or smallest first, the day's move best or worst first, or name A to Z. A header click sorts largest first, then smallest first, then back to Trading 212's order. Holdings without the figure sorted on go last either way. Phones get the same order in their cards.
- **The map** gives each holding a tile whose area is its value in the account currency, laid out largest first so each tile stays close to a square. Its colour is the **day's move** (the change since the previous close, as in the Day column) or the return **since bought** (the unrealized result against its value less that result, in the account currency, so currency moves are in it). Green is up and red is down, stronger the further it moved; the scale ends at the smallest of 1, 2, 3, 5 or 10% (the day) or 5, 10, 25, 50 or 100% (since bought) that four in five holdings stay within, so one big mover does not wash out the rest, and moves beyond it show at full strength. The text in each tile is in the text colour with its sign written out, so the colour is never the only way to read it; a holding without a figure is hatched. A tile shows the symbol, the move and the amount as far as it fits, and its full description (name, move, amount, value and share of the holdings) on hover and to screen readers; it opens the holding (a tile too small to see is skipped by the keyboard; the table opens that holding). While the day's moves are being read, the day's tiles stay neutral and show "…"; when they cannot be read, or no holding has one, the map colours by the return since bought and the day is not offered, in the map or in the order.

## The dividend calendar

The Income tab's calendar shows this month and the next 11. Each month's column is what was paid so far (solid, only this month has any) and what is expected (hatched), and the payments are listed soonest first under it. It is there while the period ends this month, as the tab's periods do.

- **Which payments.** Each holding's payments from the first day of this month a year ago, so last year's payment of a holding due again this month is still seen when it came a few days earlier. Entries within ten days of a payday's first are part of it: on the same number of shares they add up (a special dividend beside the regular one, an adjustment); on different numbers, they are one payment made on parts of the holding. A correction on its own day (zero or less) counts against the payday before it.
- **How often.** The median spacing between paydays: about a month, a quarter, half a year or a year. A holding counts as paying every week or two only when its paydays bear it out (at least six, three in four of them under three weeks apart, with entries within three days counting as one); a single payday counts as yearly.
- **How much.** The newest year's paydays (four for a quarterly payer, even when the year read holds five), per share, at the quantity held now. They are Trading 212's net payments, so after withholding tax and in the account currency. A holding bought during the year has fewer paydays than its schedule, so they are scaled up to the year's count. That year's amount is shared out over the next year's payments as the payday a year before each one was, where there was one, so a small interim and a large final dividend stay so, and as the average payday otherwise; the payments add up to it to the cent.
- **When.** The spacing stepped on from the last payday (a holding whose paydays mostly fall on a month's last day keeps to month ends). A payment due earlier this month that has not come yet is still expected, marked as due; one already paid this month is next expected a full spacing later, which for a yearly payer falls after the calendar's last month, so the calendar's months can add up to less than the expected total, and the tab says so.
- **What it does not know.** Trading 212's API gives no announced dividends, so a company that raises, cuts, skips or moves a payment shows up only once it has paid. A holding without a payday since then expects nothing. Dates are about when the payment arrives, not ex-dividend dates.

A holding's page lists its next four expected payments and how often it pays, from the same rules, and reads its dividends from the same day.

## Gain by instrument

The Performance tab's **Gain by instrument** chart, and `attribution` in `get_portfolio_performance`, split the period's gain by instrument: one bar each from a shared zero line, gains to the right and losses to the left, largest gain first, with the total above it. With more than ten instruments, the ten largest gains and losses are drawn until **Show all**. Each instrument's name opens its page, and the table view gives the figures behind each bar.

- **For a period** (1 month, 3 months, this year, 12 months), an instrument's gain is its value now, less its value at the start, less what its buys took from the account, plus what its sells paid in and its dividends. Trading 212 reports only today's holdings, so the start is the shares held then (today's, less those bought since, plus those sold since) at the quote provider's close before the period's first day, from the year of closes the holding page's price chart reads (a few days more for a 12-month period). The buys and sells are Trading 212's own account-currency amount for each fill (`walletImpact.netValue`), the dividends its net payments, and the value now Trading 212's. The period runs from the start of its first day (UTC).
- **Exchange rates.** The value at the start is converted at today's exchange rate, the one Trading 212's own valuation of the holdings implies (or, for an instrument sold since in a currency no longer held, the rate its own fills imply, which is slightly off when their account amounts include fees). So the move of the currency on what was held at the start is not in the gain; the move on what was bought since is.
- **The return** beside each bar is the gain on the money at work: the value at the start plus the buys.
- **Over all history** there is no start to value, so each instrument's gain is Trading 212's own accounting: its realised P/L on the sales, its unrealised P/L on what is held now, and its dividends. Shares bought before the history read count from what they cost.
- **What is left out.** An instrument held at the start whose closes cannot be read is listed as not counted, with the reason the price chart would give (no quote provider, no symbol, closes in another unit, or no close in the ten days before the start); one that ran out of time (closes are asked for four at a time, and no new read starts after five seconds) is read the next time, when the others are cached. An instrument whose shares do not add up (fewer at the start than none, after a split or shares moved in or out without a fill) is listed too. Without the order or dividend history back to the start (raise `maxPages`), or for a period that ends before now, there is no attribution: `attributionMissing` says which (`history`, `period`, or `data` for a read that failed) and `attributionProblem` why.

## Drawdown

The return chart's **Drawdown** view, and `timeWeighted.drawdown` in `get_portfolio_performance`, show how far the time-weighted return fell from its high. Each recorded day's value is the growth up to that day (one plus the return) against the highest growth on any recorded day up to it, less one: 0 on a day at a new high, −0.08 on a day 8% below it. Because it is read from the time-weighted return, a withdrawal is not a fall and a deposit not a rise.

- `max` is the deepest of those falls, as a negative fraction, `peak` and `trough` the days of the high it fell from and of the low, and `recovered` the first day after the low back at that high; without `recovered` it has not been made up. A day within 0.05% of the high counts as at it, since such a fall reads 0.0%. When it never fell further, `max` is 0 and there are no days.
- `current` is how far below its highest point so far it is on the last recorded day, and `currentPeak`, given only when it is below it, the day of that high.
- The benchmark's drawdown (`benchmark.drawdown`, and the dashed line) is read the same way from its return on the same days.
- It only sees the days TradePilot recorded the account value on, so a fall and recovery between two recorded days is not in it.

## Monthly returns

The Performance tab's **Monthly returns** grid, and `timeWeighted.months` and `timeWeighted.years` in `get_portfolio_performance`, give the time-weighted return in each calendar month and year the recorded days touch (UTC dates). The grid shows once two months have a return and one of them is a whole month, so not for the 1 month window, whose two part-months say no more than its chart.

- A month's return runs from the last recorded day before it to its last recorded day, chained as the time-weighted return is, so deposits and withdrawals stay out of it. With no recorded day in a month, that month is empty and the next one runs from the last day recorded before it (`from` says which).
- A month is **partial** when only part of it was recorded: the record began inside it, or its last recorded day is before its end or is today (this month, until it is over).
- A month is **extended** when it runs from a recorded day before the end of the month before it, with no reading in between (the server was off): its return takes in those days too, and `from` says from when. The text gives its days.
- The grid draws a partial or extended month with a dashed outline and its value in italics, its days in its hover text. The first month with only one recorded day has no return and is left out; the month after it is then extended.
- Each row is a year, latest first, with the year's own return at its end. Cells are tinted green for a gain and red for a loss, as the holdings map is: at full strength from the scale's end (2%, 5%, 10% or 20%, the smallest that four in five months stay within), with the value written in each cell. On a narrow screen each year is a grid of four months a row.

## Risk

The Performance tab's **Risk** panel, and `timeWeighted.risk` and `benchmark.risk` in `get_portfolio_performance`, say how much the time-weighted return moved from day to day. It shows from 20 stretches between readings. Because it is read from the time-weighted return, deposits and withdrawals are not moves.

- **Volatility** is the standard deviation of the moves (in logs) per calendar day, times √365. A stretch between readings several days apart (the server was off) counts as that many days: its move's variance is divided by its length. Weekends are recorded days that hardly move, so a calendar year's worth comes out as a trading year's would.
- **Return ÷ volatility** is the return a year at the rate over these days, over the volatility, with no risk-free rate taken off. It needs 90 days, since a return a year from fewer says little.
- **Beta** to the benchmark is how far the return moved for each 1% the benchmark moved, and **correlation**, from -1 to 1, how closely the two moved together, over the same stretches. The benchmark is in its own currency, so its currency's moves against the account's are in them.
- **Best and worst day**, **days up**, and the **Daily moves** chart count only one-day stretches, and only the ones that moved by 0.001% or more, so weekends and holidays are in none of them. The chart's bars are 0.25%, 0.5%, 1%, 2%, 5% or 10% wide, the narrowest that keeps it to 16 bars, and never straddle 0.

## Your position

The holding page's **Your position** chart draws the holding's value at each of its daily closes beside a dashed line of the net put in: the shares held at the first close at that close, plus what each buy since cost, less what each sell paid, at their fill prices. The gap between the two lines is what the price did for the position, whatever was bought or sold; the text of `get_holding_detail` gives the same. Sells for more than was put in take the net put in below zero, and the scale then shows zero.

- Trading 212 reports only today's shares, so each day's are today's less the buys after it plus the sells after it, from the year of fills the page reads (two pages of 50 orders). Fills on the first close's day count in the shares at the start. A fill counts at the close of its UTC date, so one after the close shows on the chart a day early; the gain at the end is the same.
- When older fills were not read, the chart starts on the day of the oldest fill read, since every fill after it is known, and says so.
- Amounts are in the account currency at today's exchange rate: the one Trading 212's own valuation of the holding implies, or, for one sold out, of another holding in its currency. With none, its fills' rates are used, and the note says so.
- The chart is left out, and the page says why, when going back through the fills leaves fewer than no shares on some day (shares moved out without a fill); when the closes jump by 1.8 times or more (or to 0.55 or less) in a day, or a fill's price is 1.4 times its day's close or more (or 0.7 or less), as across a split; when a fill is a split or a distribution rather than a trade; or when a fill came without a price or date. Shares moved in without a fill (a transfer in, free shares) count as held from the first close.

## The day's move

Trading 212's API gives each holding's price now but neither its previous close nor the day's change, so `get_portfolio_today` (and the dashboard, right after it loads) takes the previous close from the quote provider's daily closes, the ones the holding page's chart draws.

- **The day** is the trading day Trading 212's price belongs to, from the exchange's schedule (`metadata/exchanges`): the session in progress; for an instrument Trading 212 trades in extended hours (`extendedHours`), in the pre-market or overnight session, the day that opens next; otherwise the last session. So before the open, at night and over a weekend the move is the last session's, and the dashboard names that day ("Move on Fri, Oct 9, the last session"). Without a schedule it is today, or the Friday before on a weekend (UTC).
- **The previous close** is the provider's last close before that day. Within 6 hours of the last session's close, closes that stop short of it mean the provider has not published it yet: the holding gets no move (`missing: "lag"`) rather than two days' move. After that, the last close the provider has is taken as it is, since an instrument that trades rarely can skip a day; its date is in `previousClose` and on the dashboard's hover text.
- **The change** is the price now against that close, in the instrument's currency and unit.
- **The profit or loss** counts the shares held at the previous close from it, the day's buys from their fill price and its sells to theirs, so a holding bought today shows only what it did since the buy. The day's fills are the ones since the previous session's close on the instrument's exchange (after-hours trades count towards the next day), or since the start of the day (UTC) without a schedule; a fill price a hundred times off the close (pence against pounds) is brought to its unit. An instrument sold out on its day still counts its sells. It is converted at today's exchange rates, implied from Trading 212's valuation of the holdings, so the day's currency move is not in it. The order history is read for the newest 100 orders: when it does not come within 3 seconds, or the day has more orders than that, `fillsProblem` says so and the shares whose fills are missing count from the previous close.
- **The total** adds up the holdings with a move; its `change` is against what they were worth at their previous closes plus the day's buys. `counted` and `of` say how many have a move.

An instrument's page (`get_holding_detail`) gives the same move for that instrument as `day`, from the closes its chart reads, with its fills from the page's order history; one not held gets the change of its quoted price alone. The daily digest leads with the total and the three holdings that moved most.

The closes are cached for half an hour per instrument and shared with the holding page and the watchlist, and a previous close that will not change (the last session's, or any once 6 hours have passed since it) is kept for the rest of the day, so later refreshes ask the provider nothing for it. The read asks the provider for four holdings at a time and starts no new requests after 5 seconds; the rest get their move on the next refresh (`missing: "later"`). It waits at most 3 seconds for Trading 212's instrument list and schedules. The dashboard reads the moves after it shows, so a slow provider never holds it up, and only for a dashboard read in the last 2 minutes (not a saved result shown again); if the read fails, the dashboard says so instead of the column. The read reuses the dashboard's account read from the last 15 seconds, or joins one under way, rather than asking Trading 212 again.

## Periods

History tools accept `period` (`1w`, `1m`, `3m`, `ytd`, `1y`, `all`) or an explicit `from` and `to` in ISO 8601. `get_tax_year_summary` takes a `year` and `yearStart` instead.

They read history newest first, in pages of 50 records. Trading 212 allows about 20 history requests per minute, so each tool reads at most `maxPages` pages per data set (10 by default, 40 at most).

When the budget runs out before the requested period is covered, the result is marked `truncated`. The simple and annualized returns are then omitted, because they would be wrong.

## How the figures are defined

- **Amounts** are in the account currency, as Trading 212 reports them.
- **Realized P/L** is the sum of `fill.walletImpact.realisedProfitLoss`, which already reflects the FX rate at execution.
- **Costs** are the taxes and fees attached to each fill (commission, currency conversion fee, stamp duty, FINRA fee, and so on), plus `FEE` transactions.
- **Simple return** is `(current value − net contributions) / net contributions`. It is not time-weighted or annualized. Both returns compare against the current account value, so a window whose `to` is in the past has neither.
- **Time-weighted return** (`timeWeighted`) measures the investments, not the money paid in: it takes deposits and withdrawals out, so it is the number to compare with an index or a fund. It is worked out from TradePilot's daily value record (below), so it covers the recorded days in the window and works for any period, including ones that end in the past. Each stretch between two recorded days is measured by the Modified Dietz method: `(end value − start value − flows) / (start value + Σ flow × share of the stretch it was in)`, and the stretches are chained, `(1 + r₁)(1 + r₂)… − 1`. An empty account earns nothing, so a stretch that starts empty is measured from its first deposit, and one that ends empty up to its last withdrawal (an account counts as empty below 1 or below 1% of the money moved in the stretch): otherwise a deposit into an empty account an hour before a reading would show that hour's gain as a day's. Deposits, withdrawals and transfers between Trading 212 accounts come from the transaction history and are placed by their time. `series` gives the chained return at each recorded day and `netFlows` the money paid in less the money taken out that it leaves out. It is omitted when the transaction history read stops after the first recorded day (raise `maxPages`), and when a stretch began with nothing invested yet changed in value. Days the server was not running join into one longer stretch, and a deposit inside it is weighted by its time alone, which assumes the value moved evenly across the gap; the more days are recorded, the closer the figure is to an exact time-weighted return.
- **Benchmark** (`benchmark`) is an index fund's price return over the days of the time-weighted return: its close on or before each recorded day against its close on or before the first one, so a weekend or holiday day takes the last close before it. It is the S&P 500 through SPY by default (US symbols map to every quote provider), `BENCHMARK` on the server changes it (a Trading 212 ticker, symbol or name, or a US symbol; `none` turns it off), and `benchmark` on a call or **Compare with** on the Performance tab picks another for that read, offering the default and the ETFs held and on the watchlist (`benchmarkOptions`); `benchmarkChoice` is the one used. A symbol Trading 212 lists on several exchanges, such as VWRL, gives `benchmarkMissing: "instrument"` with its listings in `benchmarkProblem`: pick one by its exact ticker (`VWRLl_EQ`). A US symbol Trading 212 does not list (SPY, QQQ, VT) is compared through the quote provider all the same. The closes are read from a year back at least, so periods up to a year share one cached read. It is in the fund's own currency, so for an account in another currency part of the gap is the exchange rate, and a distributing fund's price leaves out its dividends while an accumulating one's includes them: for a like-for-like comparison pick an accumulating fund in the account's currency, such as `EUNL` (iShares Core MSCI World, EUR) mapped in `QUOTE_SYMBOLS`. When the provider has no closes for it, or they do not reach back to within a week before the first recorded day, `benchmarkMissing` and `benchmarkProblem` say why.
- **Annualized return** is the money-weighted rate (XIRR): the yearly rate at which every deposit, every withdrawal and today's account value net to zero. It counts when money went in, so a deposit made last month weighs less than one made three years ago. It is given for all-time windows that reach the present once the first deposit is a year old, and omitted while history is truncated.
- **Tax year** figures use Trading 212's realised P/L per sale, which is worked out against your average purchase price at the exchange rate of the sale. Tax rules match purchases to sales in their own way (the UK's same-day and 30-day rules, for example), and dividends are reported after any tax withheld at source. A sale counts in the year it filled, by its fill time; a sale without Trading 212's account-currency value is left out of proceeds and named in the notes. Use the summary to prepare and check a return; file from the Trading 212 annual statement or a CSV export (`manage_history_export`).
- **Account value history** is TradePilot's own record, because Trading 212's API reports only today's value. While the server runs, the scheduler reads the account summary about once an hour and keeps the last value of each UTC day, together with the cash free to trade and the holdings' value (`src/core/value-history.ts`). Each value is dated by when Trading 212 answered, so a read that crosses midnight UTC counts for the new day. Days it was not running have no point, and the chart spaces points by date, so a gap stays visible. Points are kept for about six years, one stored key per month. The sample account keeps its own record, and after a switch to another Trading 212 account only the current account's days are shown (the account id stays in the store). `get_portfolio_performance` returns the days inside its window as `valueHistory` once there are two or more (a date bound compares whole days, a date-time bound compares when each value was read), and the Performance tab draws them as a line with a hover readout and a table view. `get_portfolio_dashboard` returns the last 30 days of the account it shows as `valueTrend`, and the dashboard shows them as a small line beside the total value, with the change since the first of those days. The value includes deposits and withdrawals, so it is not a return.
- **Concentration** is measured over invested value only, so a large cash balance does not hide a concentrated portfolio. The flag thresholds can be overridden per call.

## Market-hours notices

Order review uses the same exchange schedules. When the market is closed, the confirmation shows a notice.

For a Market order outside an eligible extended session, the notice says that Trading 212 will queue the order and fill it at the next open, possibly at a gap price.

## Limits

Trading 212's Public API has no quote endpoint. Current prices exist only for instruments you already hold, through the positions endpoint.
